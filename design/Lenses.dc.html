What we inspect

Five lenses,
one inspection.

Every Bayrust audit uses all five. Each lens looks at your product from a different angle, so together they catch what a single kind of test would miss: from a confusing label to a payment rule that quietly breaks on salary day. Here is exactly what each one covers.

UsabilityAccessibilitySystem logicUser habitsOperational requirements

Usability

Can people finish what they came to do?

If people can’t complete the task, nothing else matters. Usability rust shows up as support calls, abandoned journeys and staff who keep a cheat sheet taped next to the screen.

  • Task completion on your most important journeys
  • Clarity of labels, instructions and error messages
  • Navigation, dead ends and loops
  • Forms: required fields, defaults and validation
  • Consistency across screens and platforms

Typical rust we find

A fee that appears only after the PIN step
An error that just says “Something went wrong”
A back button that erases everything typed

What you get

Each journey scored from start to finish, with the exact step where people get stuck.

Accessibility

Does it work for everyone?

Customers who rely on screen readers, larger text or a keyboard are locked out when the basics are missed. Accessibility is also increasingly expected by partners, funders and regulators in public and financial services.

  • Screen-reader walkthroughs with TalkBack and VoiceOver
  • Keyboard-only navigation and focus order
  • Colour contrast and text resizing
  • Labels, alternative text and error announcements
  • Every failure mapped to WCAG 2.2 AA

Typical rust we find

An amount field with no label
Grey hint text that fails contrast
A pop-up you can’t close with the keyboard

What you get

A WCAG 2.2 AA conformance summary you can share with partners and regulators, with every failure tied to its criterion.

System logic

Do the rules behave as the business intends?

Logic rust is the most expensive kind. Nothing looks broken; the product just quietly does the wrong thing, like charging twice or approving what should be rejected.

  • States and transitions: pending, paid, failed, reversed
  • Business rules and their edge cases
  • Integrations: payments, SMS and third-party services
  • Error handling and recovery
  • Permissions and roles

Typical rust we find

Success shown before the payment callback returns
A refund that can be approved twice
A role that can see data it shouldn’t

What you get

A map of every state we tested, with each contradiction and silent failure marked.

User habits

Does it fit how people really behave?

Products are designed for an ideal user. Real users share phones, switch languages, screenshot their codes and invent shortcuts. When the product fights those habits, people work around it.

  • Interviews and observation with real users or staff
  • Device, language and connectivity patterns
  • Workarounds and shadow tools: notebooks, spreadsheets, WhatsApp
  • Feature discovery and repeat use
  • Analytics review, where data is available

Typical rust we find

Staff re-typing data into a spreadsheet every evening
A key feature behind a menu nobody opens
Customers asking an agent to finish a self-service flow

What you get

A habits profile of your users, showing where the product works against them.

Operational requirements

Does it survive real conditions?

A product that works in the office can fail in the field. Network drops, shared devices, peak days and shift changes are normal conditions, not edge cases.

  • Slow, unstable and offline connections
  • Mobile-money, card and USSD payments followed end to end
  • Low-cost and older devices
  • Shared devices, sessions and sign-outs
  • Peak days such as month-end and salary day
  • Handovers between staff, shifts and branches

Typical rust we find

A draft lost when the connection drops
A mobile-money payment that succeeds but never updates the order
Sessions left open on a shared branch tablet

What you get

A conditions report showing how the product behaves in each environment we tested.

Why five

Rust rarely belongs to one lens

The worst problems sit where lenses overlap. A payment that confirms too early is a logic error, made worse by slow networks, and confusing to the customer who sees it. Looking through all five at once is how we find the cause, not just the symptom.
